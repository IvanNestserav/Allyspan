    Contributor guide

    Welcome to The Allyspan Journal

    Purpose, scope and editorial identity

    Lived experience held to the evidence

    Students, researchers, practitioners and community contributors are welcome. You do not need a university affiliation to contribute.

    The Allyspan Journal

    Research, policy, student perspectives and community voices

    A publication of Allyspan connecting academic inquiry with the realities of people navigating care, public services and legal systems. We welcome thoughtful work that improves understanding, examines inequity and offers practical, evidence-informed possibilities.

    Who may contribute

    Students, researchers, practitioners, advocates and community contributors may submit. An advanced degree, university affiliation or personal connection to Allyspan is not required. Publication depends on quality, relevance, ethics and editorial capacity. Lived experience is valuable knowledge; broad factual or causal claims still need appropriate evidence.

    Submit initially in English. If English isn't your first language, tell us when you submit - we'll discuss translation, and we publish in your language too where we can. Review takes place in English; publication in another language depends on translation and review capacity.

    What belongs here

    • Community life

      Belonging, mutual aid, organizing and collective well-being; human behavior, relationships and person-in-environment perspectives; development across the life course.

    • Systems and services

      Social welfare systems, public benefits, poverty, housing, employment and education; foundations of social work practice, ethics and engagement with individuals, families, groups and communities.

    • Justice and rights

      Race, racism, inequality, discrimination and structural injustice; Indigenous communities, sovereignty and self-determination; human rights, environmental justice and equitable access; mental health, voluntary community care, decriminalization and alternatives to incarceration; LGBTQ+ inclusion, disability, aging, HIV/AIDS-related inequities, substance-use policy, recovery, veterans and access to justice.

    Interdisciplinary and international perspectives are welcome when their relevance is explained. Work should connect clearly to Allyspan’s mission.

    Our editorial position

    We value dignity, self-determination, accessibility, anti-discrimination and honest inquiry. Critical analysis and respectful disagreement are welcome, including criticism of Allyspan. We distinguish evidence, interpretation and personal experience. Academic rigor should make an argument clearer, not harder to understand.

    What happens after you submit

    1. An editor reads your piece and checks that the submission is complete. We aim to respond within 15 business days and will communicate significant delays.
    2. If it is a fit, we work with you on revisions.
    3. Research articles require independent methodological review. Formal peer review requires at least two independent qualified reviewers whose identities are confidential to authors. We hold research submissions when qualified review is unavailable.
    4. We publish after final author approval, with the article type and actual review status stated.

    This guide sets the contributor and editorial standards for The Allyspan Journal. Choose a section to explore the requirements before preparing your work.

    Contributor guide

    What we publish

    Formats and manuscript lengths

    Word counts exclude references, tables and figure captions. Abstracts count separately. Editors may approve exceptions when justified; length alone never demonstrates rigor.

    Research Article | 2,500-5,000 words

    Original quantitative, qualitative or mixed-methods work. Include a 150-250-word abstract; introduction and question; methods; results; discussion; limitations; ethics statement; funding/conflict disclosures; and references. Report sampling, measures or interview approach, analysis, relevant uncertainty and the basis for conclusions. Human-participant work requires the documentation described in Ethics & responsibility. Methodological expertise must be available before acceptance.

    Literature or Evidence Review | 2,000-4,000 words

    Synthesize what is known, disagreements and gaps. Include a 150-200-word abstract, search sources and dates, selection approach, synthesis and limitations. A narrative review must be labeled as such. Use “systematic review” or “meta-analysis” only when the actual methods support that designation; provide search strategies and applicable reporting checklist.

    Policy Review or Policy Analysis | 1,500-3,000 words

    Identify the jurisdiction, policy instrument, status and as-of date. Explain the problem, affected groups, policy mechanism, evidence, implementation, costs or resource implications, equity effects, alternatives and recommendations. Link to primary legislation, regulations or agency material. Distinguish proposed rules from enacted law and enacted law from implementation.

    Practice and Community Analysis | 1,200-2,500 words

    An evidence-informed discussion of service design, access barriers, community initiatives or professional practice. Include context, evidence, practical implications and limitations. Program descriptions must not claim effectiveness without suitable evaluation.

    Student Perspectives / Community Voices | 800-1,500 words

    Analytical essays and reflective journals combining experience, reflection and relevant scholarship. Explain the perspective, connect it to broader systems, and identify implications. First-person writing is welcome. Label reflective work clearly; it is not original empirical research. No minimum reference count is imposed, but factual claims require sources.

    Book or Resource Review | 700-1,200 words; Commentary | 600-1,000 words

    Reviews identify the work, assess its evidence, contribution, limitations and audience, and disclose relationships to its creators. Commentaries make a focused, supported argument and acknowledge counterarguments. Letters responding to published work: 300-600 words with references where relevant.

    These labels are content categories beneath The Allyspan Journal, not separate journals. Accepted work is published on a rolling basis following review, production and author approval. Any themed collection or submission deadline will be announced separately.

    Contributor guide

    Academic writing standards

    Clear arguments, traceable evidence and accessible presentation

    Argument and structure

    State a focused question, its importance and key definitions. Use clear headings, consider counterevidence and limitations, and draw proportionate conclusions. Explain practical implications where useful. Avoid jargon and unsupported certainty.

    Sources and citations

    Use APA 7 author-date citations and matching references, with DOI links or stable URLs where available. Verify originals and quotation locators. Cite legal materials using applicable conventions and enough detail to locate the authoritative text.

    Prefer peer-reviewed research for empirical claims and primary official sources for current law, policies and rules. Use current and foundational evidence as appropriate; identify non-peer-reviewed material. News supplies context, not a substitute for stronger evidence. Sources must be sufficient, relevant and balanced.

    Precision and reporting

    Distinguish correlation from causation and statistical from practical significance. Report design, denominators, effect sizes and uncertainty where relevant; explain conflicting findings and limits. Never invent participants, interviews, quotations, results or references. Label hypothetical examples; fictional exercises are not completed research.

    Respectful language and accessibility

    Use respectful, accurate language, explain abbreviations and recognize differences within communities. Research, evidence reviews, policy and practice analyses need a 75-125-word plain-language summary; reflective pieces need a 40-75-word introduction.

    Illustrations and visual materials

    Authors may propose photographs, illustrations, charts, tables and other visuals. Identify sources and ownership or publication permissions; provide captions and descriptive alt text. The Editorial Team may accept, request changes to, replace or decline visuals based on relevance, accuracy, accessibility, privacy, quality and Allyspan’s design standards. Allyspan may propose additional illustrations. Substantive visuals and changes affecting interpretation require author approval before publication.

    Decorative illustrations must not imply that they show actual participants, clients or documented events. Disclose AI-generated or substantially altered visuals and label them where needed. Authors verify data, scales, labels and any changes to research figures. Provide editable tables and source notes; do not rely on color alone. The Editorial Team makes publication decisions; the design lead approves visual consistency, composition, colors and design quality without overriding accuracy.

    Translations and research reporting

    Translations must identify the original work, translator, permissions and review method; prior agreement and a competent reviewer are required. Submit initially in English. If English isn't your first language, tell us when you submit - we'll discuss translation, and we publish in your language too where we can. Review takes place in English; publication in another language depends on translation and review capacity. Research must follow applicable APA reporting standards and requested discipline-specific guidance; no APA endorsement is implied.

    Contributor guide

    Ethics and author responsibility

    Protect people and preserve the integrity of the record

    Originality, authorship and student work

    Submit original work and disclose prior public versions, preprints, theses and substantially overlapping publications. No simultaneous journal submission without prior agreement. All listed authors must make a substantive contribution, approve the final version and accept responsibility for their work. Provide contributor roles and acknowledge other assistance with permission. No honorary authorship or purchased papers.

    A class paper may be adapted if the author has the right to publish it and any course, university, placement or sponsor restrictions are satisfied. A grade or instructor approval is not journal acceptance. Remove course cover pages and disclose class-project origins. Do not imply university, employer or practicum-site endorsement from an author’s affiliation.

    Human participants, consent and confidentiality

    Before collecting data, authors must obtain any required institutional ethics review, approval or formal exemption determination. At submission, state the reviewing body, determination, reference number when available and consent approach. Where review was not required, explain why and who made that determination. Editorial review cannot retroactively replace required ethics approval. The applicability of research rules depends on the activity, institution and jurisdiction.

    Do not submit client records, identifying school or placement information, private messages or recognizable case histories without a lawful basis, required permissions and editorial clearance. Changing a name alone may not prevent identification. Research participation consent is not automatically consent to public publication of an identifiable story or image. No sensitive source documents or signed consent forms should be sent through ordinary submission email; editors will arrange a secure process if necessary.

    Conflicts and funding

    Disclose financial and nonfinancial interests, funding, employment, advocacy roles, relevant personal relationships and the sponsor’s role. A disclosed interest does not automatically disqualify a submission. Reviewers and editors must disclose conflicts and recuse where impartial review cannot be maintained. Authors may describe positionality when relevant without being required to disclose sensitive personal identities.

    Artificial intelligence

    Authors remain accountable for all content. AI cannot be an author. Disclose generative AI use for drafting, translation, analysis, coding or image creation, naming the tool/version when known, purpose and human verification. Routine spelling correction need not be disclosed unless it materially rewrites content. Fabricated citations and synthetic data passed off as real are prohibited. Do not upload private participant information or confidential manuscripts to AI tools without authorization and adequate safeguards. Editors and reviewers need explicit author permission before external AI processing that cannot assure confidentiality.

    Criticism must address verifiable conduct, ideas or evidence. Unsupported allegations, plagiarism, discriminatory attacks, dangerous misinformation and promotional material disguised as scholarship are grounds for rejection or further review.

    Contributor guide

    How to submit

    One complete submission package

    Submissions are open

    Prepare your complete submission, then choose Submit Your Work below Checklist & resources. Your work is sent privately for editorial review. Nothing is published automatically.

    Manuscript file

    Submit an editable DOCX, DOC, Pages, TXT, RTF or ODT file; a PDF may accompany it but does not replace the editable manuscript. Use 12-point readable type, double spacing, one-inch margins, page numbers and consistent headings. Include title, article type, abstract where required, plain-language summary or introduction, 4-6 keywords, main text and references. Place tables after the references and provide figures separately with captions and alt text.

    Separate title page and cover note

    Provide each author’s publication name, accurate role or affiliation, a 50-80-word biography, contributor roles and the corresponding author’s private email. ORCID is optional. State the word count and article type. Summarize the contribution and fit with Allyspan, disclose prior dissemination and related work, and list funding, conflicts, AI use and ethics/permission status. Do not provide a home address, student ID or unnecessary personal data.

    For an external review, editors may request an anonymized manuscript and separate identifying materials. Anonymity cannot always be guaranteed, particularly for first-person narratives or public preprints. Explain requests for a public pseudonym in advance; editors must privately verify the author and agree on accountability and safety arrangements.

    Submission route and author declarations

    Use the Journal’s private submission form to send your complete submission. Do not send manuscripts to guessed addresses or post them as comments. The receipt message must identify the policy version and confirm that submission is not acceptance.

    The corresponding author must affirm that all authors agree to submission; the work is accurate and original; required disclosures and permissions are complete; no confidential information is included improperly; and the manuscript is not under review elsewhere unless expressly agreed. Submission grants permission for confidential editorial assessment only. Publication requires separate written author acceptance of the final version and publication terms.

    Fees, payment and withdrawal

    There is no submission or publication fee and no guaranteed author payment. Any commissioned payment must be agreed in writing in advance and must not purchase acceptance. Disclose any future fees before submission; never introduce a surprise acceptance charge. Authors may withdraw before publication by written request from the corresponding author with coauthor agreement. Resolve author disputes before release.

    Before you send

    Confirm: scope and article type; word count; verified citations; accurate policy dates; limitations; accessible summary; permissions and ethics statement; author approval; conflict/funding/AI disclosures; and a clean editable manuscript. Do not include raw identifiable data. Authors under 18 require a separate safeguarding and permission process before any submission is accepted.

    Editorial review and decisions

    A rigorous process with honest review labels

    Editorial roles

    The Editor-in-Chief is responsible for publication decisions, supported by handling editors and a Community Discussion Editor for moderated responses. Roles may be combined where conflicts are managed. Subject specialists advise on relevant methods or professional issues. Founders, board members, donors and design approvers do not receive automatic publication preference.

    What happens after you submit

    1. An editor reads your piece and checks that the submission is complete. We aim to respond within 15 business days and will communicate significant delays.
    2. If it is a fit, we work with you on revisions.
    3. Research articles require independent methodological review. Formal peer review requires at least two independent qualified reviewers whose identities are confidential to authors. We hold research submissions when qualified review is unavailable.
    4. We publish after final author approval, with the article type and actual review status stated.

    Editorial review is not automatically peer review

    Ordinary publications are labeled “Editorial review.” Formal peer review may be offered only when a qualified process is actually available: at least two independent, appropriately qualified reviewers whose identities are confidential to authors, documented reports, conflict checks, author revision and an editorial decision. Describe the review model accurately for each article. Comments and letters do not become peer-reviewed merely because an editor moderates them. Only verified indexing, identifiers and institutional affiliations will be displayed.

    Reviewer criteria

    Assess a focused question, relevance, accuracy, quality and balance of evidence, sound methods where applicable, transparent limitations, ethical compliance, accessibility and useful implications. Distinguish remediable writing issues from invalid methods or missing ethics approval. Reviewers should explain specific changes respectfully; they must not demand irrelevant citations or use unpublished work for personal advantage.

    Independence, revisions and appeals

    A submission by an editor, founder, director or close associate goes to a nonconflicted handling editor; if none is available, postpone review. The author must not control their own acceptance. Send reasoned revision requests and require a point-by-point response. Acceptance and turnaround times are not guaranteed; editors communicate significant delays. Authors may submit one reasoned appeal with evidence of error or unfair process. A noninvolved editor reviews it where possible; if independence cannot be arranged, disclose the limitation.

    Visual presentation is separate from substantive review. Factual changes require editorial and author review. Editorial decisions are documented with access restricted to authorized personnel.

    Contributor guide

    Publication, rights and corrections

    Maintain a trustworthy and transparent public record

    Author rights and publication permission

    Authors retain copyright and, through a separate publication agreement, grant Allyspan a nonexclusive license to publish, host, distribute, archive and make agreed formatting/accessibility edits to the accepted work, and use attributed excerpts for Journal promotion. Translation, substantial adaptation or unrelated reuse requires separate agreement. Author-owned work must be distinguished from third-party licensed material. A Creative Commons license is applied only with explicit agreement and a rights check.

    What each article displays

    Title; article category; author and accurate affiliation or role; biography; publication and revision dates; actual review label; summary; accessible full text and references; funding, conflicts and material AI disclosures; research ethics statement where relevant; copyright/license information; and a correction/contact route. Add a suggested citation using the stable article URL. Retain version records and readable archives. A DOI or permanent preservation service is not guaranteed; any such arrangement will be identified accurately.

    Editorial independence and reader notice

    Views expressed are the authors’ and do not necessarily represent Allyspan or an affiliated institution. Publication is educational and does not establish a professional relationship or provide individual medical, mental health or legal advice. Journal activity must not be presented as authorization for Allyspan to deliver regulated services. Clearly separate research, opinion, sponsored content and organizational announcements. Journal policy prohibits candidate endorsements or opposition in Journal content published on Allyspan’s behalf; nonpartisan policy analysis remains welcome.

    Corrections and integrity concerns

    Authors must promptly report material errors. Readers may privately report concerns through the designated editorial contact, identifying the article, issue and evidence. An uninvolved editor evaluates concerns where possible, seeks an author response and documents the decision. Do not promise absolute confidentiality or retaliate against good-faith reports.

    Fix harmless typographical errors without changing meaning. Publish a dated correction for substantive changes and link it to the article. Use an expression of concern when a serious issue remains unresolved and readers need notice. Retract unreliable or seriously unethical work with a reasoned, linked notice; preserve the scholarly record where safe and lawful. Remove identifying or unlawful material when necessary, documenting the action without repeating harmful content. Offer an appeal route.

    Submission privacy

    The submission page provides the applicable privacy notice, retention information and contact route. Unpublished submissions are accessible only to authorized editorial personnel, reviewers and necessary service providers under appropriate safeguards. Private reviewer correspondence is not published without permission. Deletion requests are considered subject to legitimate recordkeeping requirements; authors should review the notice before submitting.

    Contributor guide

    Community Discussion

    Voices, questions and respectful disagreement

    “Share a question, perspective or evidence-informed response. Comments appear only after editorial review.” These standards apply wherever Community Discussion is enabled. A comment form may not be available on every article.

    Participation standards

    Suggested limit: 50-400 words per comment, with up to 3 relevant links. Respond to the article or an idea it raises; distinguish personal experience from general claims. Cite a reliable source for disputed factual assertions when feasible. Readers need not write an academic essay or disclose a personal diagnosis, service history or identity. Respectful disagreement and criticism of Allyspan are welcome.

    What will not be published

    Threats, harassment, hate speech, identifying client or participant information, doxxing, spam, commercial solicitation, impersonation, plagiarism, unsupported accusations about identifiable people, instructions for harm, or dangerous professional advice. Do not include self-harm methods or graphic detail. Do not treat comments as a confidential service request or emergency-support channel.

    Premoderation by the Editorial Team

    Every comment stays private until approved by a trained moderator. Apply the same published rules across viewpoints. Decisions: approve, request revision, decline, or escalate to the handling editor. Do not reject a relevant comment merely because it challenges the article or organization. Permit only light spelling/format edits without changing meaning; obtain consent for substantive edits. Disclose an author/editor’s relevant connection when responding.

    Moderators may close a thread, pause publication or remove a comment for a documented policy violation or safety concern. Give a brief reason where practicable and offer one request for reconsideration by another editor when available. No guaranteed response time. Remove spam or clear privacy risks promptly; do not publicly repeat the harmful details.

    Privacy and publication consent

    Collect a display name and private email for verification; publish only the agreed display name. Allow a pseudonym under consistent moderation rules. Never display an email or IP address. Minimize logs and follow the published retention notice. Before submission, obtain agreement to the comment rules and permission to display the approved comment under the chosen name. Explain that public comments may be copied by others; do not promise complete removal from the internet.

    Comments are not evidence certification

    Approval means the comment passed moderation, not that Allyspan endorses or independently verifies every statement. Editors may request sources or a formal letter to the editor for substantial critiques. Add a visible notice that the discussion is not continuously monitored and is not a route for urgent or individualized assistance.

    Contributor guide

    Contributor checklist and resources

    A final check before submission

    Manuscript checklist

    Check the article category and word limit. Include a clear argument, required abstract and summary, 4-6 keywords, headings and APA 7 references. Verify citations, quotations, statistics and policy dates. Explain limitations; distinguish evidence, interpretation and experience.

    Author and ethics checklist

    Confirm all authors’ contributions and approval; accurate roles and affiliations; corresponding-author contact; disclosure of funding, conflicts, prior versions and material AI use; research ethics documentation where applicable; consent and permissions for identifiable stories or third-party material; and no confidential client, student or participant records in the submission.

    Files and permissions

    Provide an editable manuscript, separate title page and cover note, editable tables, figure files with source/permission information and alt text. Do not send signed consent forms or sensitive raw data through ordinary email. A separate publication agreement and approval of the final proof are required before publication. No submission or publication fee is charged.

    Contact and decision routes

    Use the official submission form or editorial email displayed on the Journal website when submissions are open. For an appeal, correction, privacy concern or integrity report, use the designated editorial contact and identify the article or submission. Include a concise explanation and supporting evidence without unnecessary personal information. Do not post confidential complaints in public comments.
