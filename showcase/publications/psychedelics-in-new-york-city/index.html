    Psychedelics in New York City

    An Allyspan discussion paper and policy position · 14 November 2026

    Columbia’s research, unanswered questions, and who the evidence leaves out.

    Our publication examines psychedelic research, psychiatric disability, access and commercialisation through a community-centred lens. Read the complete paper below.

    Open the publication in a full-page reader

    About this edition

    This is a narrative review and advocacy discussion paper, not a peer-reviewed journal article or an original clinical study. Allyspan’s policy position is identified as opinion.

    The PDF includes a publication note and corrections to the original wording on trial exclusions, Allyspan’s pending incorporation and our slogan. Private drafting commentary has been removed. The remaining paper has not undergone a complete independent fact-check; time-sensitive legal, regulatory, pricing and research statements should be checked against the original sources and subsequent updates.

    Supporting sources and research context

    The paper critiques the scope and limits of the evidence. It does not establish that Columbia violated a trial protocol or ignored a specific safety concern. A small bipolar II study includes a Columbia-affiliated co-author; it is inaccurate to say all modern trials exclude bipolar disorder.

    • Columbia Psychiatry: David Hellerstein’s research profile
    • Aaronson and colleagues: bipolar II study, JAMA Psychiatry (2024)
